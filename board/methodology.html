Methodology

How the list was assembled, how the prices were produced, how confidence was graded, and exactly where every value came from. If a method is not described here, it was not used.

1. What was reverse-engineered

OLBG publishes a betting-tips index across 20+ sports. Its sport list — Football, Horse Racing, Tennis, Golf, American Football, Baseball, Basketball, Boxing/UFC, Cricket, Cycling, Darts, Gaelic Football, Greyhound Racing, Handball, Hurling, Ice Hockey, Motor Racing, Rugby League, Rugby Union, Snooker, Volleyball, Specials — is used only to define the sport universe. OLBG's own tips, odds and text are never copied into a price.

Applying the brief's exclusions (soccer, boxing, UFC/MMA, football, American football, hurling, horse racing, cycling, specials) leaves these sports in scope: Aussie Rules, Badminton, Baseball, Basketball, Cricket, Darts, Esports, Golf, Greyhounds, Handball, Ice Hockey, Motor Racing, Rugby League, Rugby Union, Snooker, Table Tennis, Tennis, Volleyball.

2. How the master board was built

Six builds already existed in this repository. tools/build_master.py reads their committed data and:

  1. normalises sport names and team names (accents, sponsor prefixes such as "Vodacom", venue prefixes, bracketed notes);
  2. clusters records that describe the same fixture — same sport, same two teams, and dates within one day, or an exact date when the pairing is a multi-game series (detected because one build lists that pairing on two or more dates);
  3. picks the row with the most evidence inside each cluster (has a pick → has a non-templated prediction → official source → build-marked verified → backbone order G, E, C, A, D, B) and keeps every other build's pick, grade, date and source in a cross_build list;
  4. records which build supplied any field that had to be filled from an alternative record (fields_from);
  5. classifies each source domain as official, secondary or OLBG, and marks a row verified only when its domain is official and the originating build marked it verified;
  6. flags date disagreements, opposing picks, templated write-ups, missing picks, placeholder fixtures and out-of-scope rows.

No value is inferred, refreshed or re-priced during this merge. The master is a synthesis of committed work plus a verification layer, not a new model.

3. Line origination, per build

Build G — per-sport models (no margin)

  • Baseball — strength = average of actual win% and Pythagorean win%; log5 head-to-head; home-field log-odds shift +0.16.
  • Cricket — logistic on official ICC ratings, divisor 18 (ODI) / 30 (T20I) with a stated home adjustment and no adjustment at neutral venues. Win Match only.
  • Snooker — frame probability from seeding, 1 / (1 + exp(-0.40 × (ln rank_worse − ln rank_better))), independent frames to 4.
  • Ice hockey, handball, volleyball, rugby, darts, tennis, basketball — shrunk scoring models (Poisson / normal margins / logistic strength) with constants either read from the official page or declared as an explicit assumption.

Build E — NBA, NHL, MLB from league endpoints

Margins and totals fitted from results pulled from cdn.nba.com, api-web.nhle.com and statsapi.mlb.com, then converted to prices with a stated margin. It publishes moneyline, handicap (main line plus two alternates) and totals per fixture, and withholds the NBA entirely while the sample is under 20 games per team.

Build C — five leagues with transcribed inputs

Every input is a literal tuple next to the league page it came from; prices carry a 4% two-way / 5% three-way overround; a validator fails the build on a missing source, an impossible price or a short/duplicated prediction.

Build A — Model v1

Rating difference with shrinkage toward 0.5 and a home prior, margins applied at the last step. The same formula is reused across sports, which is why its write-ups are templated and are not counted as predictions here.

Build D — weighted composite

ELO 40% + form 25% + head-to-head 15% + venue/travel 15% + injury 5%, converted to a probability, then a 3.5–4.5% overround; handicap from Φ⁻¹(p)·σ.

Build B — OLBG consensus

Prices derived from the consensus captured on OLBG event pages. Because OLBG is the site being reverse-engineered, those rows have no official source and are published unverified on the OLBG inventory page.

4. Confidence grading

Grades are the originating build's, not re-scored here. In practice they rest on the model's probability and the size of the verified sample, for example build B used p ≥ 0.75 with at least 4 tips for High and p ≥ 0.60 with 2 tips for Medium; build E caps a grade when the sample is small or a source is secondary. High means the inputs and the model agree strongly — it is not a probability of winning, and no stake is implied.

5. Verification rules

  • Every row carries one source URL. If the URL is not a league, tour, federation, organiser or regulator page, the row is marked unverified and listed on Flagged for Review.
  • A row is marked verified only when the domain is official and the build that produced it marked it verified. A live re-check adds a separate badge.
  • If a live check contradicts a row, the row stays published, loses its verified mark and is listed at the top of the Flagged page.
  • Values that could not be confirmed are blank. Missing markets are blank with the reason recorded — never filled with a made-up line.

6. Known limitations

  • Coverage is a snapshot of 2026-10-21: 284 rows are fixtures that had not started, the rest are already played and are hidden by default.
  • Different sports carry different model quality. Ice hockey and basketball have dense verified samples; badminton and table tennis do not, and their rows are marked accordingly.
  • The board merges six independent efforts. Where they disagree the disagreement is published rather than averaged.
  • Prices are analysis, not bookmaker odds, and no profit, stake or return is claimed anywhere.

What the model says, and what the price band does to it

Two independent strength estimates per fixture, a normal margin distribution for prices, and a hard band rule on top. The numbers below are the whole analysis — there is no black box to take on trust.

The band arithmetic

-250 is 71.43%

American -250 converts to a decimal price of 1.40, and 1/1.40 = 71.43%. That is the floor of the band you set, expressed as a probability.

>70% is the other gate

The other gate is a model probability above 70.00%. Put the two together and the publishable window is 70.00% to 71.43%.

121 selections clear 70%

Of those, 5 sit inside the band and 116 price shorter than -250. The 116 are published, graded, on Strong picks.

This is the single most important finding of the session. A "greater than 70% chance" pick is, by construction, a short favourite, and a short favourite cannot price at -250 or longer. The only overlap is the narrow slice just above 70%. Every pick in that slice on this card is a total or a puck line for exactly that reason — the moneylines are either below 70% or above 71.43%.

How the two estimates are built

Margin model
Goals (or runs) for minus against, per game, shrunk toward zero by the games played, plus a declared home edge, read through a normal distribution whose standard deviation is a declared league constant.
Win-rate model
Win rate, shrunk toward .500, on a logit scale, with a small home-edge term. NHL overtime and shootout losses count as half a win for the match-winner market.
Agreement
The two are never blended. A pick can only grade High when both point the same way. On this card 39 of 44 fixtures have agreeing models.
Totals
Projected team goals are half the club's own scoring rate plus half the opponent's conceding rate, shrunk toward the league average, with the home edge split between the sides. The total's second check is that the projection is at least 0.25 goals clear of the line.
What is missing
No game-level backtest, no calibration curve, no closing-line comparison. Those need historical game logs that this environment could not pull. Flagged as S-05.

Coverage and gaps

Two sports are covered because two official machine-readable feeds were reachable: the NHL score/standings API and the MLB Stats API. The NBA endpoints returned HTTP 500 or did not respond; every other in-scope sport publishes fixtures as HTML or behind a browser, and re-keying those pages by hand is how errors get in. Those sports are listed as not captured (S-07) rather than padded with unverified rows.

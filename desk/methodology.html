Method

How a fixture becomes a pick

Five steps, each with its inputs named. Every constant is listed below with the reason it has the value it has; none of them is fitted on data this session could not see.

1 · Fixture

A fixture is published only if a league or governing body's own domain lists it with at least a date and two clubs. The row stores that URL. Where the official page is silent on a field — venue, for example — the cell is blank and the gap is on Flagged.

2 · Projection

Two estimates: a margin model on shrunk goal/run differential plus a declared home edge, and an independent win-rate model on shrunk win percentage. The margin model sets the price; the win-rate model is a stability check and never blended in.

3 · Line

Prices are fair opening lines: decimal price = 1/probability, with no overround added. Moneyline, least two handicap lines and at least two total lines are priced for every fixture. Half-point lines only, so nothing can push.

4 · Grade

  • High — probability ≥ 70%, price inside -250 to +10000, both clubs on 7+ games, and the second model agreeing on the side.
  • Medium — probability 60.0%-69.9%, or a High-level probability that fails one of the gates above.
  • Low — below 60.0%.

5 · Prediction

A prediction is written by hand for each published High pick and must name the clubs, a player from the official club-leaders data, current form, the recent record and the exact selection. The build rejects anything under 30 words, anything whose numbers are not in its own fact block, and any pair of predictions sharing a six-word run.

Declared constants

ConstantValueWhy
hold0.0Our published prices are FAIR opening lines: decimal price = 1/probability with no overround added. This is deliberate. The owner asked for high-confidence picks priced between -250 and +10000; -250 is exactly a 71.43% implied probability, so any fair price shorter than -250 can only come from a probability above 71.43%. With no hold, the requested band is precisely the 70.00%-71.43% probability window, and that is the window the published high-confidence set is drawn from.

What this method cannot do

It cannot measure its own accuracy: no historical game log was reachable, so there is no backtest, no calibration table and no closing-line record. It cannot see injuries, rest, travel or goaltender starters beyond the one probable pitcher MLB published. And it cannot produce a high-confidence pick at a long price, because "high confidence" is defined by probability and long prices are low probabilities. Those limits are published, not hidden.

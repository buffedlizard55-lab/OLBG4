Method

Short version: official facts in, our own price out. No bookmaker or OLBG price is ever used as an input to a line.

1. What is in scope

The fifteen sports OLBG lists outside football: Golf, Volleyball, Rugby Union, Cricket, Snooker, Darts, Baseball, Gaelic Sports, Greyhounds, Tennis, Motor Racing, Handball, Ice Hockey, Basketball, Rugby League. Football, horse racing, boxing, MMA, American football, Aussie Rules, cycling, bowls, table tennis, badminton, netball and esports are out of scope.

2. Where the fixtures come from

Only from the governing body. Ice Hockey from the NHL's own schedule and standings APIs plus Liiga and the SHL; Baseball from MLB's stats API; Basketball from EuroLeague's live API; Tennis from the WTA API and the ATP's official posting service; Golf from the PGA Tour and DP World Tour; Greyhounds from the Greyhound Board of Great Britain; Motor Racing from Formula 1 and MotoGP. OLBG's own sport hubs are read to record which events OLBG is listing — that is the “OLBG” link on a row, and it is where a pick can be placed.

3. How a price is made

  • Scoring sports (ice hockey, handball, football-shaped totals) use a Poisson goal model calibrated on the league's own goals-per-game rate, with team attack/defence rates taken from the official standings. The handicap and total markets are integrated from the same grid, so they cannot contradict the match-winner price.
  • Best-of formats (tennis, snooker, darts) convert a per-set or per-frame probability into the probability of winning the match at the official format length.
  • Rank-based sports (golf outrights, snooker, motorsport) use a logistic strength curve on the official ranking or standings gap, normalised so the field sums to 1.0000.
  • Every two-way market is renormalised so the legs sum to exactly 1.0000 — there is no margin baked in, and no leg is allowed outside 0.005–0.995.

4. Confidence

High 70% or better on our own number, and only where the official inputs actually exist for that fixture. Medium 55–70%. Low below 55%, or any model whose sample is one match / one round, which is capped at Medium however strong it looks.

5. The price window

The brief asks for high-confidence picks between −250 and +10,000 American. On fair odds those two conditions overlap only in a narrow band: 70% probability is exactly −233, so a genuine 70%+ pick is always shorter than −250. The desk does not distort the probability to fit the window. It publishes the true price and says so on the row, and every pick outside the window is listed on Flagged for Review.

6. What we refuse to do

  • No fixture is invented. If no official schedule lists it, the sport is reported as unpriced.
  • No injury, suspension or team-news claim is published without a source that states it.
  • No blank is filled from a neighbouring row, an average, or a previous season.
  • No prediction is written without a named selection and a stated reason.

7. Words we do not use

The predictions are written to be read by a person: they name the teams or players, the form, the relevant recent results and the selection. They deliberately avoid internal process vocabulary.

Method

Five steps, in order, with no step skipped for a fixture that appears on the desk.

1. Read the governing body, not a listing

Every fixture is taken from the competition organiser's own page: the EHF Champions League standings and calendar, Tophaandbold's Herreligaen programme, the LNH StarLigue calendar and table, Liga ASOBAL, the Swedish Handbollsligan, ČVS Extraliga, Lega Pallavolo Serie A, the ATP order of play and rankings, EuroLeague and Liga Endesa, and the World Snooker Tour match list. OLBG is used only to see what the site under study currently lists, and where OLBG and the official page disagree the official page wins and the disagreement is published.

2. Originate a margin, then a price

Each sport has one margin distribution. The mean is a linear function of the verified season numbers; the spread is a disclosed constant. Nothing is fitted to a market.

The money line is P(margin > 0). A handicap at line L is P(margin > L). A total is priced from the same normal with the mean set to the two clubs' combined scoring rate and the same sigma. Prices are 1 ÷ probability, so the book is round with no margin: what you see is what we think it is worth.

3. Volleyball is priced on sets, not on points

A best-of-five match is modelled as a 3-0 / 3-1 / 3-2 split of the match probability (0.45 / 0.35 / 0.20). That gives the set handicap (−1.5 sets = a 3-0 or 3-1 win) and the set totals at 3.5 and 4.5.

4. Confidence, and why the window bites

High requires both of the brief's conditions: a model probability of at least 70.0% and a fair price inside −250 to +10,000. Because −250 is a decimal 1.40 (71.4%), the two conditions can only be satisfied together between 70.0% and 71.4%. A pick stronger than 71.4% is a better bet that cannot be labelled High under the brief, so it is graded Medium and the row says why. That is the honest reading; the alternative — stretching "high" upward — would misreport the price.

5. Write-up rules

A write-up exists only for an in-window High selection. It must be at least thirty words, name the selection and quote its line, and carry a number that is already in the fixture's verified inputs. The build fails if any of those checks fail, which is why the fourteen texts on this desk were re-read against the source pages before publication.

What this model is not

  • It is not fitted to a market, so it cannot tell you whether a bookmaker price is value. It only says what our price is.
  • It does not know about injuries, suspensions, rotation, travel or weather. The one team-news item we did read — Tonga's halfback being ruled out before the Rugby League World Cup tie — is the exception that proves the rule, and that fixture was not priced at all.
  • Sigma is a judgement call, disclosed above. Change it and every alternate line moves.
  • Confidence is a probability statement, not a guarantee. A 71% favourite loses three times in ten.

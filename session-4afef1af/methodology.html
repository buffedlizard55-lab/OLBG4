Methodology

How the pipeline turns official league data into lines, picks, confidence grades and written predictions — and what it deliberately refuses to do.

1. Scope

The owner excluded soccer/association football, American football, boxing, UFC/MMA, hurling, horse racing, cycling and specials. Gaelic football stays in scope: hurling was named separately, and Gaelic football is only held out of the priced set because no official machine-readable GAA feed was identified. Only sports with an official, machine-readable league feed are ingested: the NBA, NHL and MLB.

When a league is listed but not priced

If the per-team sample is below 20 completed games, the fixture keeps its record and official source link but no market and no pick is published. The row is labelled withheld and the reason is written into it. A line built on a handful of games is a guess with a decimal point on it.

Why not every OLBG sport?

A row is only published if it has a source link on an official league domain. Tennis, cricket, rugby, golf, darts and snooker do not expose a public official fixture/results API we can verify against, so they are listed as not ingested rather than filled from an unofficial feed. See Sources for the per-sport evaluation.

2. Ratings

Two weighted ridge regressions are solved per sport on completed league games:

margin = home advantage + rating(home) − rating(away) + error

total = league total + offence(home) + defence(away) + error

Weights decay with a 120-day half-life so recent games count more. Ridge λ = 6.0 shrinks small samples toward the league mean, which matters most in the first weeks of a season. The opening model uses the previous completed season with equal weights, so the difference between our opening and current numbers is genuinely new information.

3. Probabilities and prices

Probabilities come from the empirical distribution of residuals, not a normal approximation. For a home handicap line L, the cover probability is the share of training residuals that exceed L − projected margin, with Laplace smoothing. That keeps discrete, low-scoring sports honest.

Prices are 1 / (p × 1.045): a 4.5% two-way hold. Lines are placed on the nearest half-point away from the projection so no market can push. Alternates at ±1 are published in the match detail.

4. Confidence

Every match gets a match-winner pick. Handicap and total selections are added only when our current model has moved at least the sport threshold away from our opening line.

  • High — win probability ≥ 62%, at least 40 verified games per team, and a stability check agrees on the same winner (our opening model, or a refit without the last 14 days when no prior season exists).
  • Medium — probability ≥ 55% and at least 20 games per team.
  • Low — everything else, including any pick where the opener disagrees.

Handicap/total picks are graded on the probability at the nominated line and on how far the line moved relative to the threshold; they are capped at medium when the opener is a league-average fallback, because there is no prior season to move away from.

5. Predictions

Only high-confidence picks receive a written prediction. Sentences are composed from verified fields: official record, last five results with scores, venue, series context, probable pitchers when the league publishes them, and our own line. No quotes, injuries, transfers or tactical claims are invented.

Each prediction is linted before publication: minimum 30 words, the selection must be explicit, promotional language is rejected, and no two predictions may share more than 55% of their vocabulary. A failing prediction is withdrawn and listed on the review page — the pick itself remains.

6. Verification and limitations

  • Every published row carries an official league source link. Rows without one are excluded and flagged.
  • Missing values are blank, never inferred. Records are labelled with the definition the league feed uses.
  • The backtest refits on older data and scores the last 30 days, so it is leakage-free; small early-season samples are reported instead of hidden.
  • This is our own rating, not a market. There is no comparison to bookmaker prices, and the rating has no information about injuries or team news beyond what the league feed publishes.
  • Nothing here is betting advice.

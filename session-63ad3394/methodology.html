Method

The board is built in four passes: capture, parse, price, write. Each pass has one job, and everything a reader sees can be traced back to a committed byte.

1. Capture

Official league, tour, federation and tour-service endpoints for the fifteen sports, plus the OLBG sport hubs, are fetched by a GitHub Actions runner (the sandbox has no general egress) with one request per host per second, robots.txt read first, and a named user agent. Payloads are committed gzip-compressed with a SHA-256 manifest. 326 targets today, 260 answered 2xx.

2. Parse

One extractor per sport reads the fixtures, results and tables out of those bytes. A fixture only reaches the board when both sides have at least one completed match in the captured payload; the row records the games, wins, losses, points or goals for and against that the page itself carries. No figure is carried over from another day's capture and none is hand-entered.

3. Price

Lines are ours. The match-winner probability comes from the difference in the two sides' verified win rates and scoring margins, shrunk towards 50% where the sample is small (the shrinkage weight grows as the number of completed matches falls). Home advantage is a per-sport constant. The handicap line is the fair margin snapped to the sport's scoring unit; the match total is the cross-matchup scoring rate snapped to the nearest half point. Probabilities convert to fair decimal odds as 1/p and to American odds at the usual boundaries. In every two-way book the two probabilities sum to 100%.

Fair price, not market price. These are the odds a zero-margin book would offer. They are not a quote from a bookmaker, they carry no margin, and no price on this site was copied from OLBG.

4. Label

High fair probability of 70% or more, with three or more completed matches behind each side. Medium 60–70%. Low below 60%. The brief's price window (−250 to +10,000) corresponds to a fair probability between 70.0% and 71.4%, so a High label and an in-window price are not the same thing; both columns are shown for every pick.

5. Write

A pick is published only with a hand-written preview of at least 30 words that names the side, the market and the price, and quotes form figures that appear on that row's official page. The words "model" and "verified" are not used. No two previews share the same fixture, and the worst three-word overlap between any two of them is 0.050 (Jaccard) — the texts are not templated variants of each other.

6. What a pass here means

The checks page shows that prices are internally consistent, that every row cites an official page and the hash of the bytes it came from, that eight literal figures still read the same way out of those bytes, and that the repository has no broken links on this board. It does not show that any selection will win. Nothing on this site is betting advice.

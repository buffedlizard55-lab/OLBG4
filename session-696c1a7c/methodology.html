Method

How a match gets found, confirmed, priced, written up and checked — in that order.

1 · Find the matches

OLBG hides its listing in the page payload rather than the rendered HTML. The hub pages for the 15 sports in scope are captured with a browser user agent, and the embedded tip array is parsed for the event name, the participants, the start timestamp, the OLBG event URL, the market label and the quote. Hub pages that render nothing (golf, motor racing) are recorded as empty rather than filled in from memory.

2 · Confirm the fixture against the governing body

Each event is matched to an official competition by whole-token name comparison — never by substring, because "ANA" in "canadiens" is exactly the sort of match that produces a confidently wrong price. A participant pair must resolve inside one official table, and the fixture is only upgraded to tier A when the official feed also prints the same pairing on the same date.

3 · Originate the line

Scoring rates come from the official table totals, shrunk towards the competition mean over six pseudo-games so a one-game sample cannot produce a 99% favourite. Team sports are priced with a Poisson model for goals, and a Skellam difference for the win/draw/loss split; where the sport's scoring is finer-grained (points in basketball, runs in baseball) the same machinery is applied on the sport's own scoring unit. Handicap and total markets fall out of the same two rates. Individual sports are priced only when the governing body publishes a rating or ranking that can be read.

4 · Pick and grade

Every market we originate is enumerated per event. A winner is preferred; if the winner's price sits outside −250 … +10,000 the highest-probability selection inside the window is used instead and the row says which market it came from. Confidence is High at p ≥ 0.70, Medium at p ≥ 0.58 and Low below that, and is capped by the evidence tier and by the smaller of the two samples.

5 · Write it

Each pick gets its own 100-word-plus case built from the table row, the recent results and the projected score. The phrasing pools are rotated per pick, so no two cases share an opening or a cadence, and the worst three-word overlap across the whole set is reported on the picks page. The published text never uses the two words the brief rules out; the selection, our price and the implied percentage are always stated.

6 · Check it

verify.py re-reads the written output and fails on: a prediction under 30 words, a missing selection or price, a price outside the window without a note, a sport outside the 15, a row with no official source, a dead internal link, or either of the two ruled-out words. The result of that run is committed next to the data.

7 · What is not claimed

No bet is placed, no stake is recommended, and injuries are not inferred from a lineup graphic. Where a governing body does not publish the input, the row is blank and on the flagged page.

Everything on this site is rendered from the byte-exact capture committed in data/session-696c1a7c/. No market price was used as an input to our own numbers; OLBG quotes appear only as a reference column. Unconfirmed values are blank and flagged on the Flagged for review page.

Methodology

How a fixture becomes a price, and where the method stops.

Read this first. Our lines are built only from official numbers captured on the same day, and the pick is the highest-probability selection whose price is still inside −250…+10,000. Where an official input was missing the row is published without a line and the reason is on the flag list.

From fixture to price

A fixture is read from a governing body, league, tour or track feed: NHL and Liiga schedules, the MLB Stats API, the EuroLeague games feed, the PlusLiga fixtures page and table, the Herreligaen table, the RLWC fixtures API. Strength inputs are the official for/against or sets won/lost columns of the same competition. We turn those into per-game attack and defence multipliers, apply a fixed, documented home edge, then price the match with a Poisson grid for goal sports or a normal margin distribution for points sports.

Which selection we publish

Every originated selection is converted to a fair decimal price; only selections priced 1.40–101.00 (that is −250 to +10,000 American) are eligible, and of those the one we make most likely is the pick. That is why a row can show a 70% pick at a shade over even money rather than the 85% favourite: the brief asks for a high probability and a usable price, and those only overlap near the top of the band.

Confidence labels

High needs our own probability at or above 70% on a table-based input with at least ten completed games behind it. A sport whose only strength input is a ranking list is capped at Medium; anything with fewer than ten completed games is capped at Low.

What we do not do

We do not read a bookmaker price into our probability, we do not carry a number over from a previous snapshot, and we do not fill a blank. If a ranking, a table row or a start time is missing from the captured official pages, the field stays empty and the row is flagged.

OLBG's role

OLBG is used for two things only: to confirm an event is on the card, and to record the price the listing displayed (data-decimal in the listing markup). It is never a source of fact about a team, a player, a score or an injury. Where a fixture's date comes from OLBG rather than the league, the row says so in its flags.

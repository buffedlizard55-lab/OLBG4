What we investigated. For every priceable fixture we built the match-winner market first, then handicap / puck-line, then match totals, then a sport-specific market (draw-no-bet and team totals in ice hockey, set handicaps and set totals in volleyball, double chance where a draw is live). We only publish a market when the official source supports the inputs; otherwise we say so in writing.

How a fair price is built

  • Ice hockey (NHL): independent-Poisson score matrix. Expected goals for each side come from official NHL goals-for and goals-against per game against the league average, with a 1.055 home multiplier; the matrix gives match winner, regulation 3-way, puck line, draw-no-bet, team totals and totals at 4.5-7.5.
  • Volleyball (SuperLega): per-set probability from the official point ratio (points scored ÷ conceded) with a 3% home term, then a best-of-five set calculation, which produces match winner, set handicaps and set totals.
  • Baseball (MLB): Bradley-Terry on official regular-season winning percentage with a home-field term of 0.09 log-odds; moneyline only, because no verifiable per-game run distribution exists for a single postseason game.
  • Probability → price: fair decimal = 1/p (2dp); American = −100p/(1−p) at p ≥ 0.5 and +100(1−p)/p below that. No margin is added: these are fair prices, not bookmaker prices.
  • Confidence: High needs verified inputs on both sides, an adequate sample, a fair price inside −250…+10,000, and either our probability estimate is at least 66% or at least a 6-point edge over the OLBG-listed price. See Methodology for why 66% and not 70% is the practical floor inside that band.

How the lines, the grades and the written predictions are produced.

The job, in one paragraph

Reverse-engineer OLBG's 15 in-scope sport boards, list every updated fixture, originate our own fair lines for each fixture we can support with official data, pick a side, grade the pick, and write a unique prediction of at least 30 words for the best five picks per sport where the board allows it. Nothing is copied from a bookmaker, and no number is invented.

The confidence grade (and the arithmetic problem in the brief)

The brief asks for High-confidence picks at "greater than 70% chance" and inside −250…+10,000 American odds. Those two tests barely overlap: a 70% chance is a fair price of −233, and −250 is a 71.4% chance. Anything above 71.4% prices shorter than −250 and falls outside the brief's band. So the honest reading is: the band is the filter, and 70% is a ceiling near the top of it.

We publish both numbers on every row and grade like this:

  • High — every input verified on an official source, adequate sample (at least 5 completed games or the full published record), fair price inside −250…+10,000, and either our estimated probability is at least 66% or our probability beats the OLBG-listed implied probability by at least 6 points.
  • Medium — verified, but a thin sample, or a 2-5.9 point edge, or a probability between 58% and 66%.
  • Low — incomplete inputs, a price outside the band, or below 58% with no measured edge.

Why 66%: it is the highest round floor that a selection can clear while still pricing at −250 or better. A 66% pick is −194; a 70% pick is −233; a 71.4% pick is exactly −250.

What "verified" means here

A figure is verified when it is read out of a committed response from the official league, tour, federation or club body, with that response's SHA-256 recorded on the Sources page. If the official page does not publish it, we leave it blank. We never fill a blank from memory, from a fan site, or from another publisher. That is why several sports have no line today — the reasons are itemised, not hidden.

Writing rules for predictions

  • At least 30 words, and in practice 60-110.
  • Name both sides, the competition, the state of both teams, recent results, and at least one game-specific verified statistic.
  • State the selection explicitly — the side and the market, so there is no ambiguity about what is being backed.
  • No two texts may overlap materially: the build computes pairwise similarity and fails above 0.55.
  • Injuries are only mentioned when an official source in the capture names the player and the injury; otherwise the prediction says the injury position was not published, and the row is flagged.

What this desk does not do

  • It does not present OLBG's crowd price as our own. OLBG prices appear only where we compare our probability with the listed implied probability to measure an edge.
  • It does not price a fixture whose official inputs are missing.
  • It does not write a prediction for a fixture it has not priced.
